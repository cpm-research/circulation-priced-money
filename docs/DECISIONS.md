# Decisions in force

One line per rule that the model uses today, with when it was decided and why.
Every change gets a new dated line here before the code changes; a replaced
rule is deleted from this file. The git history keeps the old one. The full
earlier register, cited in the rows as "Archive", is kept outside this
repository.

"Declared" means a modelling choice, stated and accepted as such, not a
sourced fact.

| The harsh stress (decided 14 Nov): extra layoffs send the wage out of the household economy. In the distress year a layoff is extra when its uniform draw lies at or above the normal rate times its job's bracket weight, the part of the draw that the distress level adds; no new random number is drawn. The mark stays with the spell to its end, past month 36 if the spell runs on. For every spell day of a marked job, the payer still owes the wage on the job's own pay dates, in rows of their own (a wage sent outside, and when unpaid a late one). Each row is the payer's payable and nobody's receivable, and the outside is credited with it when the row is made, as outside interest and fee debt are; paying it later moves no wealth. In settlement it is a wage for the payer: fee-bearing the first time it fails, re-queued to the job's next pay date while unpaid, still owed, paid from overdue wages in the same order as late wages, and kept out of the failure share as wages are. | If same-day settlement alternates between two payment states, defer any bridge request that passes the repayment check in one state and fails it in the other while still short of money. Keep those requests out for the rest of that day. Clear the remaining requests with the usual funding order, cash reserved for due exits and pro-rata rationing, and check the result with another sweep before booking it. A deferred payer uses its existing fallback. | 6 Nov | When the pool must ration, admitting a request can cut the payments that give that very borrower enough receipts to qualify. The two day 771 failures in U had this form. In the hand case, Alice needs $100 and owes Ben $10. With $100 available, Ben needs $70 and qualifies at his $72 limit if Alice is paid in full. With Ben in the queue, Alice cannot pay him enough for him to qualify. Deferring Ben lets Alice settle and Ben uses his card for $70. |
| Settlement's sweep cap (decided 14 Nov; it raises the cap from 500).
